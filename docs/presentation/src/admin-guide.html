About this guide

This handout is for exams officers and administrators of YCT Exam Portal. It explains every screen of the Exams Office portal and walks through a complete exam period. Screens shown are illustrations with sample data; on-screen labels are quoted exactly as they appear in the app.

1

Your role and signing in

What an exams officer does in the portal, and how to get started.

As an exams officer you keep the college's academic structure and exam halls up to date, prepare each semester's session, generate the examination timetable, review and publish it, and look after student accounts. Students then see their own exams, halls and seats on their phones.

Your account

Admin accounts are created by the system administrator, not by public sign-up. Exams officers and the super admin (the first account, created during setup) have the same permissions.

Signing in

Open /login, enter your email and password, and you are taken to the Dashboard. If you followed a link to a particular admin page, you return to it after signing in.

Signing out

Use the Sign out button at the bottom of the sidebar, next to your email and role, especially on shared computers.

Running an exam period, step by step

From checking the structure to the last paper: the complete routine.

3.1 Prepare: structure, halls and session

  1. Check the academic structure
    • Schools and Departments are in place. Adding a department creates its ND and HND programmes and the four levels automatically.
    • Courses for the semester exist at the right levels, with the correct semester (First or Second) and credit units. Use Upload CSV for many courses at once (section 5).
  2. Set up the halls
    • For every venue in use: name, code, real seating capacity, latitude and longitude, a short description, and Active.
    • Capacity drives seating; the coordinates place the hall on students' maps and walking routes.
    • Set halls that are not in use to Inactive. They are left out of new timetables and hidden from students.
  3. Create and activate the session
    • On Timetable, click +, enter the start year and choose the semester, for example "2025/2026 First Semester".
    • Make sure the session is Active so students can see it; set older sessions to Inactive.
  4. Open the registration window
    • Ask students to sign up at /register. Straight after sign-up they see their level's courses for the semester, all pre-ticked, and tap Complete Enrollment.
    • Follow progress on the Students screen and the Dashboard's Total Students.

3.2 Generate and read the report

  1. Generate the timetableWhen registration has closed, open Timetable, choose the session and click Generate. The dialog has three fields:
    FieldWhat to choose
    SchoolAll Schools, or one school. When you generate for one school, halls already booked by other schools' timetables are respected.
    Exam Start DateThe first exam day (next Monday by default).
    Exam Period1 to 6 weeks (2 weeks by default). Monday-Friday slots, 8:00-10:00 AM and 12:00-2:00 PM, are created automatically: 2 weeks gives 20 slots.

    Generating again replaces the existing draft entries for the session (or for the chosen school).

  1. Read the report"N courses scheduled across M time slots" confirms the draft. Two lists appear only when something needs attention:
    • Unresolved: no clash-free slot was left for these courses. Choose a longer Exam Period and generate again.
    • Hall Overflow: not enough hall space for these courses. Add halls or capacity (or activate a hall), or choose a longer exam period, and generate again.

3.3 Review, adjust and publish

  1. Review department by departmentUse the tabs: All and one per department, each with its number of exams. Check the columns Code, Title, Level, Date, Time, Halls and Status (Draft) for each department.
  2. Adjust with Edit SlotOn a draft row, Edit Slot opens Move to Different Slot: choose a slot under Select Time Slot. The move is checked for clashes, and halls and seats in the new slot are re-assigned automatically.
  3. Publish AllClick Publish All and confirm. The system first checks that every scheduled course has a hall, every enrolled student has a seat, and every course with enrolled students is on the timetable. When published, students with the session Active see their exams, halls and seats immediately.
  4. Share Seat AssignmentsOnce published, Seat Assignments lists every student's course, date, hall and seat, ready for invigilators.

3.4 After publishing

Published exams are locked

Published rows show Locked instead of Edit Slot, and students can no longer drop published courses.

Late registrations are seated

A student who enrols after the timetable was generated automatically receives the next free seat in one of that course's halls, when there is room.

Bigger changes: Reset All

To restructure a published timetable, click Reset All and confirm, then Generate and Publish All again.

Visibility

Switching a session to Inactive hides it from students; switch it back to Active to show it again.

Exam-period checklist
  • Schools, departments and courses checked
  • Halls: real capacity, coordinates, Active
  • Session created and set to Active
  • Older sessions set to Inactive
  • Students told to register and choose courses
  • Registration closed
  • Generate: School, Exam Start Date, Exam Period
  • Report read; Unresolved / Hall Overflow cleared
  • Every department tab reviewed
  • Publish All
  • Seat Assignments shared with invigilators
  • Students reminded to check Navigate before exam day

Managing students

Find any student, correct their record, reset a password or remove an account.

TaskHow
Find a studentType a name or matric number in the search box, and narrow by All Schools → All Departments → All Levels. Results come 50 per page.
ViewView opens the student's profile: details, department, programme, level and enrolments, with the same Edit, Reset password and Delete actions.
EditOpen the row menu (⋯) → Edit: Full name, Matric number, Email, and Department → Programme → Level. Matric numbers are stored in capitals and must be unique; a changed email also becomes the student's sign-in address.
Reset password⋯ → Reset password: enter a New password and Confirm new password (at least 8 characters), then give it to the student privately.
Delete⋯ → Delete and confirm. The student's record, enrolments, seats and sign-in account are removed.
Tip

If a student registered in the wrong department or level, Edit their record rather than asking them to register again. Their sign-in stays the same. They can then update their courses on the Courses tab.

Importing data from CSV

Add many departments or courses at once from a spreadsheet.

Save the spreadsheet as CSV (comma-separated values) with a header row, then use Upload CSV on the Departments or Courses screen. Header names are matched without regard to capitals, and quoted fields may contain commas. After each upload an import report lists what was imported, skipped or rejected, with the line number and reason for every rejected row.

Good to know

Published timetables stay stable: a course that is already on a published timetable keeps its semester and level when a file is imported. To move such a course to another semester or level, use Reset All first.

6

Good practice

Habits that keep every exam period smooth.

Keep only the current session Active

Students then see exactly one session's timetable and courses, with no confusion between semesters.

Enter real capacities and coordinates

Accurate seat numbers keep classes in halls that fit; accurate coordinates give students the right walking route.

Generate after registration closes

Class sizes then match real enrolments, so halls and seats are right first time. Late registrations are still seated automatically when there is room.

Review every department tab

Look through each department before Publish All; use Edit Slot for small adjustments.

Deactivate rather than delete halls

Set halls you no longer use to Inactive: they leave new timetables and the students' list, while past records stay intact.

Choose a comfortable exam period

Two weeks gives 20 slots. If the report lists Unresolved courses, a longer period gives the engine more room.

Share Seat Assignments early

Once published, pass the list to invigilators so they can check hall and seat at the door.

Sign out on shared computers

Use Sign out in the sidebar footer when you leave an office computer.

7

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to the questions exams officers ask most.

What does Hall Overflow mean?

There was not enough free hall space to seat the whole class for the listed courses. Add a hall, increase a hall's capacity, activate an inactive hall or choose a longer exam period, then click Generate again.

What does Unresolved mean?

No clash-free slot was left for the listed courses within the exam period. Choose a longer Exam Period in the Generate dialog and generate again.

How do I change a published timetable?

Published exams are locked. Click Reset All, confirm, then Generate and Publish All again. Small changes are best made with Edit Slot before publishing.

A student forgot their password.

Find them on Students, open the row menu (⋯) and choose Reset password. Enter and confirm a new password of at least 8 characters and give it to the student privately.

A student registered in the wrong level or department.

Choose Edit on their row and set the correct Department → Programme → Level. Their sign-in stays the same; they can then update their courses on the Courses tab.

Can I generate for one school at a time?

Yes. Choose that school in the School field. Halls already booked in a slot by other schools' timetables are respected, so schools can be scheduled one after another.

Why can't students see the timetable yet?

Students see only published timetables for Active sessions. Check that you clicked Publish All and that the session's switch shows Active.

A student enrolled after I generated. Do they have a seat?

Yes, when there is room: they automatically receive the next free seat in one of that course's halls. The seat appears in Seat Assignments and on the student's timetable.
